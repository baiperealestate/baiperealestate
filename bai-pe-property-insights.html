Thinking About Selling Your Property in Curaçao?

What every property owner should know before getting started.

Read the Guide

Selling a property is an important decision. For some owners, it is part of a new chapter. For others, it may be connected to an investment, a family decision or a change in personal circumstances. Whatever your reason, it helps to understand the process before putting your property on the market.

Every property and every situation is different. The right approach depends on your goals, the condition and location of the property, the current market and the practical steps involved in a sale.

This guide explains some important points to consider when preparing to sell property in Curaçao.

Before You Decide to Sell

Before thinking about photographs, advertisements or an asking price, take a moment to consider what you want to achieve. Having a clear reason and a realistic timeline can help shape the decisions that follow.

You may already have a preferred selling date, or you may simply be exploring your options. You might need to sell within a certain period, or you may be willing to wait for the right buyer.

These details can influence how the property should be presented, priced and marketed.

A first conversation does not have to mean that you are committing to sell. It can simply be an opportunity to discuss your situation, understand the possible next steps and decide whether selling now makes sense.

02

What Is Your Property Really Worth?

One of the first questions property owners ask is: “What could I sell my property for?”

It is an important question, but the answer should be based on more than a number that feels attractive or an asking price seen in an online advertisement.

An asking price is not necessarily the final amount a buyer will pay. A useful pricing discussion considers the property's location, size, condition, layout, land or ownership rights, available documentation and the properties buyers can choose from at that time.

Factors That Can Influence the Price

  • Location, accessibility and surrounding area
  • Land size, built area, layout and functionality
  • Overall condition, maintenance and quality of finishes
  • Ownership structure and relevant property documentation
  • Comparable properties and competing listings
  • Buyer demand and the timing of the sale

Pricing too high can reduce interest and cause a property to remain on the market longer than expected. Pricing too low may mean that you do not achieve the result you were hoping for.

The aim is to find a considered market position that supports your goals while remaining realistic for potential buyers.

A Helpful Distinction

A market-position discussion with a real estate agent is not automatically a formal valuation or an appraisal for a bank, court or other official purpose. If you need a formal valuation, you should engage a qualified valuation professional.

03

7 Things to Consider Before Putting Your House on the Market

A little preparation can make the selling process clearer and help buyers understand the property. You do not necessarily need to renovate everything. Focus first on the details that matter most.

  1. Take an honest look at the property's condition. Identify visible maintenance issues and decide which repairs are practical before listing.
  2. Make the spaces easy to understand. A clean, tidy and well-presented home helps buyers focus on the property itself.
  3. Gather important documents. Collect the information you have about ownership, land, permits, plans, utilities and relevant agreements. A notary can advise which documents are required.
  4. Be clear about what is included. Make a list of items that will remain, such as appliances, fixtures or other agreed items.
  5. Consider tenants or existing arrangements. If the property is rented or occupied, clarify the agreements and practical arrangements before setting viewing expectations.
  6. Think about your timeline. Consider when you would ideally like to receive offers, complete the sale and hand over the property.
  7. Agree on a clear selling strategy. Discuss the asking price, target buyers, marketing approach, viewing arrangements and how enquiries will be handled.
04

Selling Your Property Without a Real Estate Agent: What Should You Consider?

Selling privately is an option. It can give an owner direct control over communication and viewings, and it may avoid an estate agency commission. However, the owner also takes responsibility for the work and decisions involved in bringing the property to market.

This can include researching the market, deciding on an asking price, preparing the property, creating advertisements, responding to enquiries, arranging viewings, assessing offers and coordinating with the notary and other parties.

Before deciding, consider how much time you can dedicate to the process, how comfortable you are negotiating and whether you have a clear plan for checking prospective buyers and managing the transaction.

If you choose to work with an agent, ask what the service includes, how the property will be marketed, how often you will receive updates, what the commission and other costs are, and what the agreement requires from both parties.

You should understand the terms before signing.

05

Why Good Property Marketing Matters When Selling Your Home

Good marketing is not simply about putting a property online. It is about presenting the property clearly and accurately so that the right people can understand what is being offered and decide whether it suits their needs.

Good Presentation Starts With the Essentials

  • Clear, well-composed photographs that represent the property honestly
  • A description that explains the property's features without exaggeration
  • Accurate information about location, dimensions, layout and relevant details
  • Exposure through suitable online channels and networks
  • Consistent communication when potential buyers ask questions

Every property has something different to offer. Effective marketing identifies the features that matter to likely buyers and communicates them in a professional, straightforward way.

It should create interest without making promises that the property cannot support.

06

Selling Property in Curaçao: Understanding the Local Process

The details of a property transaction depend on the property, the parties and the agreements made. In general, a sale involves several stages.

1

Initial Discussion and Preparation

Clarify your goals, gather available property information and discuss a suitable selling approach.

2

Pricing and Marketing

Agree on the market position, prepare the property information and begin presenting it to potential buyers.

3

Viewings and Offers

Arrange viewings, respond to questions and consider the terms of any offer, not only the proposed price.

4

Written Agreements and Notarial Process

Once terms are agreed, the parties document the arrangements. The notary guides the legal checks and prepares the required deed.

5

Transfer and Handover

The transfer is completed through the applicable notarial process. The parties coordinate payment, the deed and practical handover arrangements.

In Curaçao, the transfer of ownership of real estate is generally completed through a notarial deed and registration in the public registers. The notary can explain the legal requirements, documents, costs and timing that apply to your specific sale.

Ownership, long lease rights, existing mortgages, tenants and other registered or contractual matters can affect the process. Do not assume that every property sale follows exactly the same timeline or has the same requirements.

07

What Happens After You Accept an Offer on Your Property in Curaçao?

Accepting an offer is an important step, but it does not necessarily mean that the sale is complete. The next steps depend on the terms agreed by the parties and the legal and practical checks required for the transaction.

Typically, the parties will need to confirm the agreed terms in writing, provide the required information and documentation, and work with the notary on the transfer.

Make Sure the Agreement Is Clear About

  • The agreed purchase price and payment arrangements
  • Any conditions, deadlines or other commitments
  • What is included in the sale
  • The intended transfer and handover date
  • How outstanding matters will be handled

There may also be costs associated with the transaction. Which party pays particular costs can depend on the agreement and applicable rules. Ask the notary to explain the costs and responsibilities before you commit to the terms.

Keep communication clear and respond to requests for documents in a timely manner. Your real estate agent can help coordinate communication and keep you informed, while the notary handles the legal aspects of the transfer.

08

Choosing the Right Guidance When Selling

Selling a property is not only a transaction. It is a decision that can involve your finances, plans and personal circumstances. The person guiding you through the process should take the time to understand what matters to you and communicate clearly along the way.

When speaking with a real estate agent, look for transparency about pricing, marketing, communication, commission and the services included. You should feel comfortable asking questions and understand what will happen next.
